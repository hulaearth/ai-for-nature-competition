1. Register your team

  1. Choose a team of two or three people. Open Register and sign in with your own Hugging Face account.
  2. The captain creates the team and invites teammates. Teammates sign in with their own accounts and confirm their invitations.
  3. Wait for organizer approval. Confirmed members can upload using a shared team allowance. Only the captain chooses the final model.

2. Download the essentials and check your setup

The Data page explains the downloads. Start with the 76.1 MB modeling tables and 28.2 MB encrypted final tables. Larger spatial inputs and the optional companions are available if you need them; tables alone are a complete route.

Keep files on the local disk of the machine that will run your model, verify SHA256SUMS and retain the notices. The data controls show current organizer release status.

Before 11:00, prepare only. Download, unpack, check formats and licenses, and test accounts, packages and compute with unrelated synthetic examples. Exploration, feature creation, training, tuning and visual analysis start at kickoff, including work by AI assistants.

Rehearse an upload

Once approved and practice is open, download the neutral 120-row practice template. In Submit, select Practice, choose the CSV and click Submit prediction. Encryption is automatic. Keep the receipt and check for Accepted. Practice has ten accepted checks, no AP score, and is allowed before kickoff.

3. After 11:00, build a first working model

Run the supplied CPU starter first. It learns from the training examples; the separate German development set gives every team the same held-out comparison. The default uses 64 AlphaEarth values summarized over a 1 km radius. Save its result, then change one important choice at a time.

Focus on a working prediction pipeline and a presentation explaining your method, experiments and limitations. The two additional tasks are optional; insights from your main modeling work count in the same judging categories.

4. At 13:30, make the final predictions

  1. Use the release controls on Data to get the age unlock key. Open and verify your preloaded final inputs using the starter helper.
  2. Predict all ten species at all 15,915 anonymous locations, using the same environmental inputs as your trained model.
  3. Export row_id,score with exactly 159,150 rows. Match every supplied sample ID once, use finite scores between 0 and 1, and keep the CSV below 24 MB. Row order does not matter.

A CSV is a plain-text table. Keep the supplied identifiers so every score can be matched to its request. A score ranks locations; it is not a calibrated probability that a bird occupies a place.

5. Submit and check your final choice

  1. Open Submit, sign in with your confirmed team account, select Final and provide the decrypted sample CSV when requested for local ID checks.
  2. Enter your method/report and exact code/checkpoint reference, choose your prediction CSV and click Submit prediction. The page validates, encrypts and delivers it automatically.
  3. Keep the provider receipt and check the entry status. A receipt confirms delivery; Accepted confirms validation. A rejection includes a reason to fix.
  4. As captain, leave “Make this my final entry if accepted” selected, or use Use for final beside an accepted entry. Check the highlighted Final choice before 17:30.
Only the captain chooses the final model.

Confirmed teammates can upload, but a teammate upload does not choose the final entry. Without an explicit choice, the latest accepted captain upload is the fallback. If only teammates uploaded, the captain must select an entry. Rejections preserve the earlier choice.

You have five accepted final predictions for the whole event, shared across delivery routes. Selecting an already accepted entry uses no extra attempt. Final private evaluation uses that same chosen prediction; organizers do not pick your best hidden result.

Leave time to check acceptance.

The original unedited Hugging Face server receipt binds your exact request and encrypted file fingerprint. Its timestamp controls eligibility even when evaluation finishes later. Keep the receipt and contact an organizer if an entry remains pending.

6. Upload your presentation

Below the prediction controls in Submit, use Upload your presentation and sign in to the Google Form. The captain enters the approved team slug and captain Hugging Face username. Submit a PDF up to 10 MB; an editable PowerPoint source up to 100 MB is optional.

Keep the confirmation page. The latest complete valid captain response before the deadline is used. Files remain organizer-private; you do not need editing permission for Challenge data. The prediction and presentation use separate receipts.

Plan for five minutes of presenting and two minutes of questions. Explain your question, method, German development result, supported findings with visualizations and limitations. Include the method/code reference and reproducibility details.

Technical notes, when you need them

Choosing tables, spatial patches or rasters

The prepared tables are a complete starting route. Try a regularized linear model or suitable tree model, and compare local versus wider environmental features. Make sure file joins are correct and preprocessing respects the training/development split.

Optional spatial patches preserve the landscape's arrangement. They have 64 environmental channels and validity masks, not RGB colors. Follow the documented decoding, normalization and focal offsets; ordinary color preprocessing and brightness augmentation do not automatically make sense. Measure memory use and final prediction time, and download the matching final patches if your model needs them. Keep a tested table model as a fallback.

The 100 m and 1 km features summarize circular neighborhoods; they are not single coarse pixels. Native vectors, context summaries, 10 m patches and 40 m patches describe different views of the environment. A park may be lost in a broad average, so compare views rather than assuming one resolution is best. Fine pixels do not remove uncertainty in bird coordinates.

The 148.57 GB German rasters are optional advanced inputs. Custom features must also be possible to produce from the anonymous final inputs. See the data page for sizes, formats, species and licenses.

Comparing models fairly and using other species

Keep a short experiment log: question, features, split, seed, runtime and result. Everyone reports the common German development score. Five-fold spatial cross-validation is optional; it is not required and earns no points just for being run. Fit learned preprocessing and helper stages inside the permitted training partitions and follow the same exclusions.

After model selection, you may refit on training plus development data. Declare it, retain the earlier held-out development score and keep the same final prediction interface.

Task 2 offers optional exploration of German records, such as coverage, sources or seasonality. Task 3 asks whether other species help prediction. For a cross-species claim, compare with and without helpers using identical target labels, backgrounds, splits and feature supports. Learn helpers within the permitted training partitions, using out-of-fold predictions or another construction that avoids leaking answers, and predict them from the supplied final inputs. Observed helper labels at final locations are not supplied. A well-controlled negative result can be useful.

Learning data covers Germany; final evaluation checks transfer to nearby Central Europe. Do not download additional bird observations, range maps or pretrained bird-distribution predictions. Additional environmental training data are allowed, but final predictions must be computable from the supplied anonymous inputs. Organizers will not prepare extra test features on request.

What to include in your presentation

Explain your question, method, common German development result, supported findings and limitations. Include useful details about features, preprocessing, helpers, any final refit, and AI/API use. Declare compliance with the permitted-data and preparation rules.

Use appendix slides for reproduction details or links to code, configuration and dependencies if you want to support the reproducibility points. Link the method report and exact code/checkpoint in Submit and retain the CSV hash. Keep source and license notices with derived work. The rules page explains the five reproducibility checks and the full scoring breakdown.

Explain uncertainty and reporting bias. Background examples represent matched recording opportunities, not confirmed bird absences; unreported species remain unknown. Scores rank reported seasonal presence rather than measuring the probability that a bird occupies a place. Keep the limitations of annual embeddings and uncertain coordinates clear in your presentation.
